- **Choices that came from the research:**
  - **One message per person naming only them, the tenant's sending address as ORGANIZER, `RSVP=FALSE`**: Outlook treats an organizer's own copy differently and sends no meeting response when RSVP is not TRUE; directors' addresses stay private.
  - **REQUEST and CANCEL, never PUBLISH for invites:** only these give update and cancellation semantics in Outlook. PUBLISH only for "Add to my calendar".
  - **A random UUID as the UID** (RFC 7986 §5.3), not the meeting id or a domain.
  - **ical4j with tzurl updates switched off and the VTIMEZONE built from `java.time`:** the updater is a run-time call to a hosted service (AGENTS §3.1) and has hung servers; the app's own zone data must describe the invite.
  - **RSVP in the app, no answer links in email:** unsigned replies "may not be trusted" (RFC 6047), clients disagree on sending them, link scanners fetch links (RFC 8058).
  - **The secretary records answers given by email or phone** (OnBoard's community shows secretaries cannot); **only a new date or time asks again** (OnBoard resets on any change).
  - **The register records what CGI's guidance lists:** in person, remote, alternate, in attendance, apologies, absent, and who was not there for the whole meeting.
  - **Alternates follow the company-law model** (UK model articles art. 25–26): only while the member is absent, once per person, for the number only, never as chair or independent, and never for an item the member is recused from.
- **E04's open question answered:** a recused member's alternate does **not** stand in for that item, and is recused from it with them (ADR-0064). Source: art. 26(2) "subject to the same restrictions as their appointors"; the opposite practice (New Hampshire) uses a pool of alternates, not personal ones.
- **Changes from the overview, recorded in ADR-0064, to report to the owner:**
  - the quorum override is a **committee-type setting** (reviewed by a second administrator), not a tenant setting, and marks the decision "to be ratified at a quorate meeting" (Robert's Rules);
  - an alternate counts for the number only. Today's `Quorum.evaluate` lets an alternate's seat meet a "chair or vice-chair" condition and an "independent" condition, and lets one person count for two seats; T4 changes that.
- **Found while reading:**

  - `meeting_audience`'s source CHECK allows MEMBER, GUEST and ATTENDEE; T3 widens it for ALTERNATE.
  - `Notifications.notify` returns nothing; the invitations panel needs the outbox id, so T1 returns a `NotificationResult`.
  - E04's reminder on the working day before and E05's meeting reminder would arrive the same morning; T6 merges them into one message.
  - E05's migrations are `meeting/V5`–`V7` (after E03's V2 and V3 in progress and E04's V4 planned), `forum/V6` and `notification/V4`.
  - The ADR index check needs E04's ADR-0062 (PR #173) merged before this plan's ADR-0063 and ADR-0064 pass on `master`.

- **Spike findings (ical4j 4.4.0 and biweekly 0.6.8 on Java 25.0.5):**
  - Both run on Java 25. With `ical4j.properties` setting `net.fortuna.ical4j.timezone.update.enabled=false`, writing, validating and parsing in many zones asks for no connection (`NoNetworkTest`, a refusing `ProxySelector`). The default is `true`.
  - **ical4j's validation applies its iTIP rules by METHOD** (a REQUEST without ATTENDEE or ORGANIZER fails). The separate `ITIPValidator` reported nothing in the spike. ical4j accepts a CANCEL without ATTENDEE or with `STATUS:CONFIRMED`, and a PUBLISH with ATTENDEE, so `InviteRules` adds RFC 5546's table for the methods memo sends. ical4j does not check that a TZID has its VTIMEZONE either; `IcsFolding` does.
  - **Given a `ZonedDateTime`, ical4j writes the local time in its own bundled, stale zone data.** A 10:00 meeting in America/Sao_Paulo came out as 11:00, because Brazil ended daylight saving in 2019. The writer passes `java.time`'s local time with the TZID parameter, and the VTIMEZONE comes from the JVM. The second parser and the cross-zone test caught it.
  - **ical4j folds by characters, not octets** (an Arabic title gave lines of 128 octets). The writer folds at 75 octets itself, between characters.
  - **ical4j escapes TEXT correctly, but its parser misreads a backslash before a line break.** The tests read text values back with biweekly.
  - **A backslash in a quoted parameter value is read as escaping the closing quote** (ical4j's parser; a CN of `;\` swallowed the next line). Names lose backslashes, double quotes and carets.
  - **ical4j's `TimeZone` adapter misreports zones whose standard offset changes** (Antarctica/Casey), although it picks the right observance. Its choice of observance can also switch up to a day early before a change in zones far west of UTC (America/Adak), with the same VTIMEZONE that works for America/New_York. The property oracle therefore leaves out instants within two days of a change. The exact change instants are checked when each VTIMEZONE is built: the RRULE dates, expanded by ical4j's `Recur`, must equal the JVM's transitions.
  - **biweekly assumes one hour of daylight saving.** In Australia/Lord_Howe (30 minutes) and Antarctica/Troll (2 hours) summer time it misplaces times, so there only ical4j is compared with `java.time`.
  - **ical4j registers zone ids of its own (`ical4j~…`) in `ZoneId.getAvailableZoneIds()` as it parses**, and they have no rules. Only tests parse; the generators leave them out.
  - **The VTIMEZONE:** a zone with no change in the window around the meeting (one year before to one year after; Asia/Dhaka) has one STANDARD observance from 1970. A zone whose yearly rules govern the window has one observance per rule with a yearly RRULE, starting the year before the window opens (Europe/London: `BYDAY=-1SU`, the form clients know). A zone whose rules change within the window (Antarctica/Casey, Africa/Casablanca) gets explicit observances without RRULE (narrowed after CI, see below). TZNAME is numeric (`+06`, `+0530`).
- **Deviations from the plan, recorded here:**

  - The design's "board-level credit preset" does not exist (the presets are Board, Board committee and Management committee). Every preset uses the name and never overrides a quorum; a neutral title is set per type.
  - `CLASS:PRIVATE` goes with a neutral title, as ADR-0063 says, until the recorded run (T9) shows whether clients accept it.
  - The reply instructions name only the answers the meeting's mode offers (in person, online, or both).

## 2026-11-18 (T1, after the first CI run)
- **CI found a wrong reading in `InviteWriterProperties`** (run 44333621213): a Europe/Volgograd meeting on 1 January 2020 at 04:00 local (+04) was read by biweekly at +03, an hour late.
  - Our file was correct by RFC 5545. It had a STANDARD observance from 28 October 2018 at +04 and one from 27 December 2020 at +03. ical4j and `java.time` agreed on +04.
  - biweekly 0.6.8 ignores the onsets of observances without RRULE and applies the last one's offset. America/Caracas (‑04:30 until May 2016) showed the same.
  - A calendar client could do the same, so **the writer changed, not the test**. For zones whose offsets change by one-off decisions (no yearly rule covering the window), the VTIMEZONE now lists only the observance in force at the start and any change during the meeting. The last observance is then the one in force. Changes outside the meeting say nothing about it.
- **The property tests then found a second biweekly quirk** (Africa/Cairo, whose October change at 24:00 cannot be said in RRULE, so it is explicit). With a lone DAYLIGHT observance, biweekly had no standard offset to add its hour to and read the time two hours off.
  - A VTIMEZONE without STANDARD is also unusual for clients (Outlook expects one). When the observance in force is daylight saving, the standard one before it now comes first.
- **Handled in the test, with the reason in `InviteChecks`:** a meeting across a one-off change (a change during the meeting, in a file without RRULE). There biweekly still reads the start at the later observance's offset, which no single VTIMEZONE can avoid. Its start is compared with nothing but ical4j, which agrees with `java.time`. Daylight saving that is not one hour (Lord Howe, Troll) stays as before.
- **Added as fixed examples** (`InviteCrossParserTest`, every method through both parsers):
  - Europe/Volgograd 2020-01-01, and a meeting across its change on 27 December 2020;
  - Europe/Samara 2011-06-01;
  - Asia/Pyongyang 2016-06-01;
  - America/Caracas 2016-01-01;
  - Africa/Juba 2020-06-01;
  - Africa/Cairo in summer and in winter 2027.
- **New property assertion:** without yearly rules, the last observance is the one in force at the start (or at the end, after a change during the meeting). The coverage check now spans the window only for yearly or fixed observances and the meeting itself otherwise.
- **Checked:**
  - a one-off sweep (not committed) of every JVM zone at quarterly dates from 2016 to 2027 through both parsers: 28,986 cases, no failure;
