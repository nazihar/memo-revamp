## 3. Minute-taking practice (R5)

### 3.1 CGI (then ICSA), *Minute taking* guidance note, April 2017
<https://cgi.org.uk/media/mpjeexxj/minute-taking.pdf>, read in full.
- **§3 Purpose:** "an accurate, impartial and balanced internal record of the business transacted"; "as a minimum … the key points of discussion, decisions made and, where appropriate, the reasons for them and agreed actions, including a record of any delegated authority".
- **§5.3.2 Level of detail:** "Minutes should not be a verbatim record. They should summarise the key points of discussion and, especially, key challenges that have been raised, but focus on the decision". The usual wording is "It was resolved that …" for a board and "It was agreed that …" for a committee. "The purpose of minutes is to record what was done, not what was said". "If the board or committee requires action to be taken, the minutes should make clear who has responsibility for the action and the date". Papers for noting: "received … and its contents noted".
- **§5.3.2 Actions:** "We recommend the inclusion of actions in minutes"; "Many respondents maintain a separate actions schedule … An updated actions schedule is presented to each meeting … and often discussed under 'matters arising'" (E07).
- **§5.3.2 Papers:** papers are retained "with, although not normally as part of, the minutes"; some organisations include "copies of items approved by resolution".
- **§5.3.3 Naming names:** naming is "increasingly common in the financial services sector (especially banking)". It "will normally be appropriate to name individuals who": present a paper; are charged with actions; "have declared a potential or actual conflict of interest"; "abstain from a vote or recuse themselves"; "request that their name be noted as dissenting".
- **§5.3.4 Dissent:** "individual directors may request that their dissenting view be recorded in the minutes. It is normal to comply with such requests"; the chair may direct that dissent is recorded; some record the reason, some do not. Specimen: "The board agreed to y, with Mr Z requesting that his dissent be recorded." Dissent may be corrected at approval, but raising it for the first time then is "a new matter arising … The rewriting of history should not be permitted."
- **§5.3.6 Conflicts:** the minutes make clear that the conflicted director "took no part in that section of the meeting and, where applicable, could confirm that the meeting remained quorate" (E04 research §3.3).
- **§5.3.8 Tips:** record breaks and the closing time; state for each item whether there was a paper and whether it was circulated in advance or tabled; "bold capitalisation of key words such as APPROVED, DECIDED, RESOLVED, NOTED"; "each item should show whether the directors agreed/resolved/noted/received the item".
- **§6 Draft minutes:** circulate "as promptly as possible after the meeting … examples quoted to us ranged from two days to a week"; "Most organisations have a process whereby the draft minutes are agreed with the chairman before circulation to others"; drafts "clearly marked 'draft'".
- **§6.1 Editing:** the process should be "agreed and recorded"; the secretary may pass technical sections to executives for comment; "In the event of conflicting views on the draft minutes, the chairman should be the final arbiter"; guard against attempts to "rewrite history".
- **§6.2 Post-meeting developments and amendments:** "It is not normally appropriate to alter the original minute"; one model: before approval, typographical errors corrected by the secretary and minor corrections made at approval; "After approval, minor typographical errors may be corrected by the company secretary, but any other corrections must be made in 'matters arising' at the succeeding meeting", with a cross-reference on the signed copy.
- **§6.3 Approval:** "It is the practice of virtually all organisations for the minutes of a meeting to be approved or otherwise at the succeeding meeting. This is not a legal requirement … where meetings are infrequent, it may be appropriate for the finalisation of minutes to be expedited through the use of email or other technology. However, even where this is the case, it would seem reasonable for the approval of the minutes to be ratified at the succeeding board meeting."
- **§6.4 Conflicted directors' access:** split practice, "a strong bias towards redaction from larger companies, particularly those in the financial services sector", and the middle view "entitled to know the decision, but not any detail of the discussion" (ADR-0062 follows it).
- **§7.1 Access:** auditors and regulators may need the minutes; sections not to be released "can be marked so that when they are prepared for external review redaction is simple".
- **§8 Retention:** "The Act requires that corporate board minutes be retained for at least ten years … our recommendation has always been that they be retained for the life of the organisation"; "a PDF copy of the signed hard copy" as a back-up. §8.1: most secretaries destroy their notes once the minutes are approved; notes kept are discoverable.

### 3.2 The law on minutes as evidence (UK Companies Act 2006)
- s.248: "Every company must cause minutes of all proceedings at meetings of its directors to be recorded", kept "for at least ten years from the date of the meeting".
- s.249(1): minutes "if purporting to be authenticated by the chairman of the meeting or by the chairman of the next directors' meeting, are evidence … of the proceedings at the meeting"; s.249(2): the meeting is then deemed duly held until the contrary is proved.
- Sources: <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/section/249/data.htm> **[snippet]**, <https://legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/part/10/chapter/9/crossheading/records-of-meetings-of-directors> **[snippet]**.
- **What this means for us:** a decision takes effect when it is made at the meeting; the minutes are its evidence, and their authentication is by the chair of the meeting or of the next one. Nothing in the law ties a decision's effect to the minutes' approval.

### 3.3 South Asian secretarial practice (ICSI Secretarial Standard SS-1)
SS-1 (India) is the most specific published standard on the timetable and is close to Bangladeshi bank practice. Read through a practitioner summary (<https://taxguru.in/company-law/minutes-board-meeting-companies-act-2013-secretarial-standard-1.html>); the ICSI page itself refused the connection (<https://www.icsi.edu/media/webmodules/publications/FinalSS1.htm> **[snippet]**).
- Draft minutes circulated "within 15 (fifteen) days from the date of the conclusion of the Meeting"; directors comment "within seven days from the date of circulation"; a director who does not comment is deemed to have approved the draft; late comments at the chair's discretion.
- Entered in the minutes book "within 30 (Thirty Days)"; altered after that only with the board's express approval at a later meeting.
- "The Chairman shall initial each page" and sign the last page; for electronic minutes "the Chairman shall sign the Minutes digitally"; after signing they "shall not be altered".
- Pages "consecutively numbered"; minutes preserved "PERMANENTLY".
- "Extracts of the Minutes shall be given only after the Minutes have been duly entered", but certified copies of a resolution may be issued earlier if its text was placed before the meeting.
- Signed minutes are circulated to all directors within 15 days of signing.

### 3.4 Bank supervisors
- **Basel Committee, *Corporate governance principles for banks* (2015), ¶60:** the board "should maintain appropriate records (eg meeting minutes or summaries of matters reviewed, recommendations made, decisions taken and dissenting opinions)", available to the supervisor (E04 research §3.1; <https://www.bis.org/publications/201507-guidelines-corporate-governance-principles-banks.pdf>).
- **Bangladesh Bank, BRPD notice of 4 August 2025:** board and committee minutes must capture "All deliberations, opinions, observations, recommendations, and dissent notes related to agenda items"; a director's "Note of Dissent" on an agenda item "must be comprehensively recorded"; "If an observer from Bangladesh Bank is present at a meeting, their comments must also be incorporated into the minutes". It recalls BRPD Circular No. 02 of 11 February 2024 on board meetings, which many banks had not complied with ("Key discussion points and directors' remarks were often missing"). Reported by The Business Standard (<https://www.tbsnews.net/economy/banking/bb-mandates-recording-dissenting-opinions-board-meetings-1204361>) and The Financial Express (<https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/trade/all-dissents-observations-must-be-recorded-in-board-meeting-minutes>, E04 research). The circular's own text was not found online.
- **Bangladesh, committee minutes to the board:** a listed bank's corporate-governance compliance statement says its audit committee's minutes "must be ratified in the next meeting of the Board of Directors" (The Premier Bank, <https://thepremierbankplc.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Status-on-Compliance-of-Corporate-Governance-Code-1.pdf> **[snippet]**). E06 does not build committee-to-board reporting; the board's agenda can carry the approved minutes as a paper (E03).
- **Extracts used by banks:** account mandates ask for a certified "extract of resolutions … a true copy of those passed by its board" (Standard Chartered Bangladesh mandate form, <https://www.sc.com/en/uploads/sites/66/content/docs/CB_Mandate_Integrated_Generic_Bangladesh_2020_v1.pdf> **[snippet]**). Inside a bank, credit operations act on the committee's approval before the minutes are signed; the extract is how they receive it.

### 3.5 Resolution numbering
- No published standard for numbering board or committee resolutions was found. Practice seen in bank forms and practitioner guides is a reference to the meeting ("Board Meeting No. …, dated …") and a register kept by the company secretary (<https://treelife.in/compliance/board-resolution-formats-for-company/> **[snippet]**).
- The overview's example `BRC/2026/014` (committee code, year, serial) matches what ADR-0044 already lets a memo type express (text, a code, year or financial year, a padded sequence). The format should therefore be configuration on the committee type, with the committee's code in place of the memo type's.
- A register that proves no number is missing is what ADR-0044 built for memos (numbers in order, voids shown, a count check).

## 5. When a decision takes effect, and when the memo moves on
- **The law and practice:** a decision is made at the meeting; the minutes are its evidence (s.249). Extracts of resolutions may be certified before the minutes are signed when the text was before the meeting (SS-1). Banks' credit operations act on committee approvals before the next meeting.
- **The risk in acting at once:** a mistake in recording the outcome would move the memo on. A short buffer helps: the decision is held while the meeting is in progress and released when the secretary marks the meeting as held.
- **Options:**
  1. **At the end of the meeting** (and at once for a decision recorded later): memos move on the day of the meeting; the minutes confirm.
  2. **When the minutes are approved:** the safest record, but a credit approval waits two to six weeks for the next meeting.
  3. Per decision, chosen by the secretary: flexible, but a choice made under pressure in the room.
- **Recommendation:** a committee-type setting with option 1 as the default and option 2 available for a committee whose charter says decisions take effect on confirmation. (ADR-0065; question 1 in `ux-review.md`.)

## 6. Design questions answered by the research

### 6.1 Minutes approval: workflow engine or a fixed path (changes the overview)
The overview planned "approval through the workflow engine as a new `minutes` subject". The research shows the approval path is a fixed governance sequence, not a route through people:
- chair agrees the draft (CGI §6), members comment (SS-1, BoardPro), and the minutes are approved at the next meeting by the committee's own decision or by circulation, then authenticated by the chair (CGI §6.3, s.249);
- the next-meeting step is a decision of another meeting, not a task; the comment window is not a task either (silence is consent);
- every reader of a draft needs their own redacted copy (ADR-0062), and the engine shows its subject whole to every task holder;
- the configurable part CGI asks for (technical review by executives, §6.1) is covered by the secretary asking named people for comments, which does not block.

Options:
1. **A fixed path in the minutes module** (draft → chair review → circulation → approval at the next meeting or by circulation → the chair's confirmation), chosen per committee type, shown in "My meetings" and by notification.
2. The workflow engine with a `minutes` subject, a definition per committee type, and new engine features: assignees by committee role, a wait for the next meeting, a wait for a comment window, per-task redacted views.

**Recommendation: option 1** (ADR-0066; question 3 in `ux-review.md`). It has fewer moving parts for an on-prem install, matches the practice found, keeps one place for directors (the meeting page and "My meetings"), and leaves the engine unchanged. E07's action-item closure can still use the engine.

### 6.2 Narrative or action minutes
CGI recommends neither verbatim nor bare action minutes: key points, challenges, the decision and its reasons, and actions. The committee's own template decides the style; the default template has, per item, "Key points discussed", the decision in capitals (APPROVED, NOTED, …), the resolution number and text, conditions, dissent and abstentions, declarations and who left the room.

### 6.3 Notes during the meeting
OnBoard, Convene and BoardPro let the secretary type per-item notes during the meeting, which fill the draft. CGI §8.1 notes that secretaries' notes are discoverable and often destroyed after approval. **Recommendation:** an optional notes box per item on the decision screen; its text fills the item's "Key points discussed" in the draft; notes are visible only to the secretaries, are kept with the meeting (never deleted, AGENTS §3.3), and are not part of the record (question 5 in `ux-review.md`).

### 6.4 The recused reader's copy of a Word document
Redaction must be reliable on a document a person edits freely. Options:
1. **One locked content control per item** (tag `memo-item:<itemId>`), written around each item's block when the draft is generated; the copy for a reader replaces the control's content with a generated block (heading, their declaration, the decision). A check at each save and before each step finds a missing or duplicated control and stops the step with a plain message.
2. **Item headings in a dedicated style**, with an item running to the next such heading. No control to lose, but a heading whose style is changed silently merges two items.
3. **Separate documents per item**, joined at approval. Reliable, but secretaries lose the single document they know, and page numbering and formatting across joins are poor.

**Recommendation: option 1, proved by the T1 spike** (ONLYOFFICE keeps the tag and lock through edit, save and reopen, and Word through download and upload; undo removing a control is caught by the check). If ONLYOFFICE drops tags, option 2 is the fallback, with the same check. The secretary previews any reader's copy before circulating ("See the copy for M. Hossain").

### 6.5 Numbering resolutions (R9, changes the overview)
The overview planned "one numbering service" moved out of the memo module with expand → migrate → contract. Options:
1. **Share the format code, not the counters:** the pure parts, periods, rendering and checks move to `com.memo.common.numbering`; resolutions get their own counter table in the minutes module with the same locked-row pattern. No memo table or number changes. Meeting numbers already work this way (E03).
2. **One numbering module** owning every counter, with memo counters migrated into it (expand → migrate → contract over two releases), and every caller going through it.

**Recommendation: option 1** (ADR-0065). It meets every requirement (gapless, voids recorded, configurable format, a register with a count check) without touching a single existing memo number, which was risk R8. One counter table per owning module keeps each module's migrations independent (ADR-0006).

## 7. Usability for older, non-technical directors and secretaries
- **Directors:** never see a workflow; they see "Minutes to read" on the meeting page and in "My meetings", read their own copy in the app, and either say "No comments" or "Suggest a correction" in a box. The chair has one more button, "Approve the minutes", with the usual step-up.
- **Secretaries:** record each decision in a form beside the agenda, with the outcome as large buttons in the committee's own words ("Approved", "Approved with conditions", …); the item in progress is preselected (E05); the quorum check and override come from E05's dialog; vote counts and names are an optional "Record a vote" section. They get a real Word document they can format.
- **Words, not codes:** "Resolution BRC/2026/014 will be numbered when the meeting ends"; "The memo will move on when you mark the meeting as held"; "This copy hides item 5 from M. Hossain, who declared an interest".
- **Nothing irreversible without a warning:** releasing a decision to a memo and approving minutes say what cannot be undone.

## 8. Conclusions for the design
1. A **decision per item**, recorded by the secretary: the committee type's outcome, the resolution text, structured conditions, an optional validity date, an optional vote with counts, named dissent with an optional reason, named abstentions, "took no part" from recusals, and E05's quorum snapshot. Corrections are new entries until the minutes are approved (ADR-0065).
2. **Resolution numbers** per committee and year or financial year, in the committee type's format, taken in agenda order when the meeting is marked as held (and at once for later decisions), from a locked counter row; voids recorded; the format code shared with memos, the counters not (ADR-0065).
3. **Back to the memo** when the meeting ends by default, or when the minutes are approved, per committee type; DEFERRED, REFERRED and WITHDRAWN mapped to the existing submission and workflow effects; the resolution number on the memo's note sheet and so in its final record (ADR-0065).
4. **Minutes** drafted from the committee type's .docx template (block loops added to ADR-0048's engine), one locked content control per item, edited in ONLYOFFICE, approved along a fixed path (chair review, circulation, approval at the next meeting or by circulation, the chair's authenticated confirmation), locked as a PDF/A-2u record; recused and restricted readers get generated copies; corrections annexed; resolution extracts (ADR-0066).
