# E05 Timers, SLAs and escalation — log

## 2026-11-13
- Plan written under the delegated gate (Phase 2 Q1): research (R7 timers at volume, business calendars), ADR-0041, design, threat model, tasks T1–T8, test plan, UX review notes. Implementation starts with T1.
- Choices worth knowing:
  - **Timers:** one db-scheduler execution per timer, with a `workflow_timer` row as the source of truth. Firing re-checks the task, so a stale timer does nothing.
  - **Calendar:** one business calendar per tenant, with weekly periods, dated exceptions and a time zone. Changes apply to tasks created afterwards.
  - **Escalation:** runs through the engine as the workflow system actor, with the E04 resolution and separation-of-duties rules.
- Out of E05 scope, recorded for later:
  - several calendars per tenant (per branch or country);
  - pausing an SLA while a task waits for an answer;
  - SLA reports and dashboards (Phase 7 reporting);
  - recalculating due times when the calendar changes.
- **T1 (business calendar):**
  - **Arithmetic:** `workflow.calendar.BusinessCalendar` is pure and DST-safe. Periods are local times; lengths are measured between the instants they map to.
    - In working time a **day is a working day**: the same time of day on the n-th working day after. Hours and minutes are working minutes. So `P1D` from Thursday 15:00 in Dhaka is Sunday 15:00, and `PT16H` is Monday 15:00.
    - SLA durations are therefore `SlaDuration(days, minutes)`, not `java.time.Duration`, where 24 hours would turn into a day. The property test caught this.
    - A start outside working hours counts from the next working period. Due times landing on a period's end stay there (17:00, not 9:00 the next day).
    - Walks are bounded to 3 years. A calendar with no working days gives no due time.
  - **Storage:** `workflow_calendar` (weekly periods per weekday, version) and `workflow_calendar_day` (a date with its own periods, or none: closed, and a note), in migration `workflow/V11` with forced RLS.
  - **Deviation from the design:** there is no time-zone column. The calendar uses the tenant's time zone setting (`OrgDirectory.zone()`), so there is one source of truth.
  - **Default:** until set, Sunday–Thursday 9:00–17:00.
  - **API** `/api/v1/admin/workflow/calendar` under `workflow.calendar.manage` (new, privileged):
    - GET and PUT for the week, with If-Match;
    - `GET /days?from&to` (at most 400 days);
    - `POST /days` for one day or a range of up to 366 days, closed or with its own hours;
    - `DELETE /days/{day}`;
    - `POST /preview` returns the due time for a start and a duration.
  - At most 4 non-overlapping periods a day. Audit: `workflow.calendar.updated`, `.day_set` (from, to, closed), `.day_removed`.
  - Also here: `OrgPerformanceIT`'s plan check now rules out bitmap scans too. CI's planner chose one on PR #62; it is a separate commit.
  - Tests: `BusinessCalendarTest`, `BusinessCalendarProperties` (Dhaka, London, New York and UTC; due inside a working period, additivity, working days), `BusinessCalendarIT`, matrix `p2-e05-timers.yaml`.
- **T2 (SLA model and due times):**
  - **Definition:** `sla` gains `remind` (`before`, and `after` with up to 3 entries) and `escalate` (`after`, `action` notify / reassign / nextLevel, and `to`, an assignee). This is schema 2, additive; `workingTime` defaults to true.
  - **Validation:**
    - a reminder before the due time must be shorter than it (`SLA_REMIND_BEFORE`, comparing a day as 24 hours);
    - `reassign` needs `to` (`SLA_ESCALATE_TO`);
    - `to` is never the initiator and must exist (E04 references).
  - **`workflow_task.due_at`** (migration `workflow/V12`, with a partial index on open tasks) is set on decision tasks of SLA steps when the task is written. It is computed by `DueTimes` from the action's time with the tenant calendar, which is read once per action and only when needed.
    - A task replacing another on the same step (reassign, forward, staff change) keeps the step's due time.
    - A step that runs again (send-back, resubmit, re-resolution) gets a new one.
  - **Where due times show:**
    - task lists: `TaskItem.dueAt`, the inbox's `InboxItem.dueAt`, and the admin open tasks' `dueAt` with a new `filter=overdue`;
    - the memo view's `dueAt`: the earliest due time of the current step's open decisions (`WorkflowQueries.dueOf`).
  - SLA JSON moved to `SlaJson`; the definition printer was at its complexity limit.
  - Tests: validator cases, `SlaDueIT` (working and calendar time, kept on reassign, renewed after a send-back, none without an SLA).
- **T3 (timers):** `workflow_timer` (migration `workflow/V13`, forced RLS, foreign key to the instance) holds one row per reminder, due notice and escalation of a decision task on an SLA step.
  - **Scheduling:** each row has one db-scheduler execution (`workflow-timer`, data `(tenantId, timerId)`), scheduled in the action's transaction. `WorkflowTimers.plan` runs after the writer:
    - new decision tasks on SLA steps get their timers;
    - a task closed as reassigned or transferred passes its pending timers to the task replacing it on the same step;
    - every other closed task's pending timers are cancelled. Cancelling marks the rows only; the executions then find them cancelled and do nothing.
  - **Times:**
    - the reminder before the due time counts back in calendar time, and is skipped if it would already be past;
    - reminders after and the escalation count on from the due time in the step's working or calendar time.
  - **Firing** locks the row and acts only if it is still pending and its task is still open; otherwise it marks the row cancelled. It then publishes `WorkflowTimerFired` (ids, kind and ordinal). T4 tells people, and T5 acts on `ESCALATE`. Retries and suspended tenants follow the `Jobs` conventions.
  - `runDue()` fires due timers by the clock; tests construct `WorkflowTimers` with a `MutableClock`.
  - Tests: `TimersIT` (order and once each, cancelled on close, passed on with reassignment, a stale execution does nothing).
- **T4 (reminders and overdue notices):** two new notification kinds, `TASK_REMINDER` and `TASK_OVERDUE`, in the workflow category. Mail templates are in English and Arabic with the reference and link only; in-app labels are in English and Arabic.
  - `MemoNotifier` answers `WorkflowTimerFired` for memos: a reminder (before or after the due time) tells the holder, and the due time tells the holder and the initiator.
  - Escalation timers are left to the workflow (T5).
  - A task nobody holds (blocked `UNRESOLVED`) reminds nobody; its blocked notice already went to the reassigners.
  - Tests: `MemoReminderIT` (reminder, then overdue to the approver and the initiator, in-app and by email, no subject in the emails).
- **T5 (escalation):** engine command `Escalate` (`Escalations`) and action `ESCALATE` (migration `workflow/V14`; audit `workflow.task.escalated`; web history "Escalated by the workflow: overdue", en/ar).
  - **Move** (`reassign`, `nextLevel`): closes the task as reassigned and creates its successor. The successor is a blocked `UNRESOLVED` task when nobody is left. The timers move with it, and the step's due time is kept.
  - **Notify:** the holder keeps the task, and an `Escalated` effect publishes `WorkflowEscalated` per target.
  - **Targets** come from `TimerEscalations`:
    - `notify`: the named assignee's people, else the holder's supervisor;
    - `reassign`: the first person the named assignee resolves to;
    - `nextLevel`: the holder of the position the holder's position reports to.
    - The initiator and the holder are never targets.
  - **Actor:** the workflow itself (`TimerEscalations.WORKFLOW_ACTOR`, a fixed id that is no user); the audit actor is the timer's system actor.
  - **Lock order:** an escalation timer takes the subject and instance locks before its own row, the order actions use, so it cannot deadlock with a concurrent decision.
  - A task that moved on in the meantime is refused by the engine, and the timer still counts as fired.
  - The memo tells escalation targets cleared for it: they become participants and get `TASK_OVERDUE`.
  - Tests: `EscalationIT` (next level once even when run twice, as the workflow and audited; notify to a named person; the initiator is never a target, which gives a blocked task).
