## 1. Starting point (code, 2026-11-18, `master` at ebe9531 plus the E04 plan on `phase4/e04-plan`, PR #173)
- **Meetings (E03 T1, #169):** `meeting` with `starts_at`, `ends_at` and an IANA `time_zone`, `mode`, `location`, `join_url`, `join_details`, `classification_id`, `status` (DRAFT → SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS → HELD, or CANCELLED; MINUTES_APPROVED and CLOSED are E06's) and `version`. Times change only through `reschedule`; place, mode and link through `PATCH`.
- **The audience** (`meeting_audience`, materialised): MEMBER rows for every roster seat in office on the meeting's date (position seats resolved to holders), GUEST rows, ATTENDEE rows for item attendees, each with `members_only`, `until` and `removed_at`. The CHECK allows only MEMBER, GUEST and ATTENDEE. E03 design §1.6 leaves "alternates standing in for members who sent apologies" to E05.
- **Notifications:** `MeetingNotifier.cancelled` sends `MEETING_CANCELLED` to the audience in the change's transaction. Its comment says "E05 adds the calendar invitations".
- **Mail (ADR-0030):** `Mail.enqueue(template, to, params, locale)` writes `notification_mail_outbox` (parameters encrypted with `DataKeys`) and schedules the `mail-send` job in the same transaction. `MailDelivery` builds a `MimeMessageHelper(mime, multipart = true)` with a plain-text and an HTML body. **There is no attachment or calendar-part support.** Each tenant may have its own relay (`notification_smtp`, password encrypted, step-up to change, a test-send action, a delivery log); the platform relay is the fallback (`MEMO_SMTP_*`). Subjects take no parameters. Categories are SECURITY (mandatory), ADMINISTRATION, WORKFLOW and WATCHING.
- **Dev mail:** the dev VM and local Compose send everything to Mailpit 1.31.5 (`deploy/compose/compose.yaml`, `deploy/ansible/site.yml`: "Mail goes to Mailpit on the VM (owner decision 2026-10-25)"). No real client ever sees a message from dev.
- **Quorum (ADR-0057, `forum/Quorum.kt`):** `QuorumRule` (`counts`, `basis`, `seats`, `threshold`, `minimum`, `required` role sets, `composition`, `alternatesCount`, `remoteCounts`, `recusedCount`), `QuorumSeat` (member id, role, voting, attributes), `QuorumPresence` (`remote`, `byAlternate`, `recused`) and the pure `Quorum.evaluate(rule, roster, attendance)`. Two behaviours matter for E05:
  - an alternate attending for an absent member makes the **seat** present, so the seat's role satisfies a `required` set and the seat's attributes count for `composition`;
  - one person can appear as an alternate for more than one seat, because presence is keyed by seat, not by person.
- **Forum members (ADR-0056):** each seat has an optional `alternateUserId` ("attends and counts when the member sends apologies", flow K3). `ForumDirectory.membersOn(forum, date)` returns it.
- **Recusal (E04 plan, ADR-0062, not merged):** a person is recused from item *i* when they hold a RECUSED or AWAITING_RULING declaration on *i* or its parent. `Recusals` (`isRecused`, `recusedFrom(itemId)`, `ofMeeting`) and `DeclarationChanged` are published for E05. E04 runs `Quorum.evaluate` with everyone present for a planning warning and leaves live quorum, alternates and departures to E05. ADR-0062 says: "whether a recused member's alternate stands in for that item is E05's question" (E04 log: "no source found").
- **Migrations:** `meeting/V1` is merged. E03 reserves `meeting/V2` (agenda, T2) and `meeting/V3` (revisions, T3); E04 reserves `meeting/V4` (declarations). The forum module is at `forum/V5`, the notification module at `notification/V3`. E05 therefore starts at `meeting/V5`, `forum/V6` and `notification/V4`.

**Library choice (ADR-0063):**
- **ical4j 4.4.0** for writing and validating. It is actively maintained, BSD-3, has iTIP-aware validation, and its model covers VTIMEZONE, ATTENDEE parameters and METHOD. Two settings are mandatory: `timezone.update.enabled=false` (no outbound call to tzurl.org; AGENTS §3.1 forbids runtime SaaS, and the call can hang) and validation left strict on output, so a malformed invite fails the job instead of reaching a director.
- **biweekly 0.6.8 as a test-only second parser.** It is BSD-2 and works, but has one maintainer and no release since January 2024, so it is not on the runtime path. In tests it re-parses every golden file and every generated invite, so a writer bug that ical4j's own parser forgives is still caught by an independent implementation.
- **VTIMEZONE built from the JVM's zone rules**, not from ical4j's bundled tzurl definitions. The meeting's UTC instants are computed with `java.time` (E03), so the invite must describe the same offsets. ical4j's bundled set can lag the JVM's tzdata, and its updater is switched off.

## 3. iCalendar, iTIP and iMIP (R3)

### 3.1 What the standards require
**RFC 5545 (iCalendar)** <https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5545>:
- §3.6.5: "An individual "VTIMEZONE" calendar component MUST be specified for each unique "TZID" parameter value specified in the iCalendar object."
- §3.8.4.7 `UID`: "The "UID" itself MUST be a globally unique identifier"; it is "the method for correlating scheduling messages with the referenced "VEVENT"".
- §3.8.7.2 `DTSTAMP`: required in VEVENT; with a METHOD it is "the date and time that the instance of the iCalendar object was created"; "The value MUST be specified in the UTC time format".
- §3.8.7.4 `SEQUENCE`: "When a calendar component is created, its sequence number is 0. It is monotonically incremented by the "Organizer's" CUA each time the "Organizer" makes a significant revision". It also says that a change of sequence "is not the mechanism that an "Organizer" uses to request a response"; the `RSVP` parameter is.
- §3.8.1.11 `STATUS` for VEVENT: TENTATIVE, CONFIRMED or CANCELLED.
- §3.1: "Lines of text SHOULD NOT be longer than 75 octets, excluding the line break", and lines end with CRLF.
- §3.3.11 TEXT: commas, semicolons and backslashes "MUST be escaped with a BACKSLASH character", and line breaks are written as `\n`.

**RFC 7986 §5.3** <https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7986> updates UID: the RFC 5545 advice to use host and domain names "is no longer considered good practice"; "UID values MUST NOT include any data that might identify a user, host, domain, or any other security- or privacy-sensitive information. It is RECOMMENDED that calendar user agents now generate "UID" values that are hex-encoded random Universally Unique Identifier (UUID) values".

**RFC 5546 (iTIP)** <https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5546>:
- §2.1.4: SEQUENCE "MUST be incremented" for changes to DTSTART, DTEND, DURATION, DUE, RRULE, RDATE, EXDATE or STATUS; "The "SEQUENCE" property value MUST be incremented each time the "Organizer" uses the "ADD" or "CANCEL" methods"; it is not incremented for REPLY, REFRESH, COUNTER, DECLINECOUNTER or a delegation REQUEST.
- §2.1.5: "The primary key for referencing a particular iCalendar component is the "UID" property value"; the highest SEQUENCE "obsoletes all other revisions of the component with lower values"; with equal SEQUENCE, "the "DTSTAMP" property is used as the tie-breaker".
- §3.2.2: "The "UID" and "SEQUENCE" properties are used to distinguish the various uses of the "REQUEST" method": a higher SEQUENCE is a reschedule (§3.2.2.1); the same SEQUENCE is an update of details, not a reschedule (§3.2.2.2).
- §3.2.2.7: the Organizer "sets the "ATTENDEE;RSVP=TRUE" property parameter" to ask for a reply.
- §3.2.5 CANCEL: METHOD CANCEL; DTSTAMP, ORGANIZER, SEQUENCE and UID required; "MUST include some or all Attendees if the entire event is cancelled"; STATUS "MUST be set to CANCELLED to cancel the entire event. If uninviting specific Attendees, then MUST NOT be included"; "When a "VEVENT" is cancelled, the "SEQUENCE" property value MUST be incremented". Messages "related to a previously canceled event ("SEQUENCE" property value is less than the "SEQUENCE" property value of the "CANCEL" message) MUST be ignored" (§4.2.9).
- §3.2.1: PUBLISH is "an unsolicited posting"; Attendees "MUST NOT be present". REQUEST "is an explicit invitation to one or more Attendees".
- Errata: four verified or reported, none on CANCEL or SEQUENCE (<https://errata.rfc-editor.org/rfc5546>).

**RFC 6047 (iMIP)** <https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6047>:
- §2.4: the `text/calendar` part "MUST also include the MIME parameter "method"", whose value "MUST be the same (ignoring case) as the value of the "METHOD" property"; a part without it "is not considered to be an iMIP body part"; the charset "MUST have the value "UTF-8""; scheduling messages "SHOULD be sent with an alternative, human-readable form"; "multipart/alternative" "MUST NOT be used to represent two slightly different iCalendar objects".
- §2.3: the email "Sender" "may not equal that of the "Organizer""; the relevant address is read from the calendar part, and ORGANIZER and ATTENDEE are `mailto:` URIs.
- §2.2 and §3: the originator "must be authenticated by a recipient", by S/MIME; "Unauthenticated messages (i.e., unsigned messages) may not be trusted". In practice none of the clients below requires S/MIME; an unsigned reply from a mail client is exactly that kind of untrusted message.

**Taken from the standards:**
- One random UUID per meeting as the UID, kept for life; never the meeting's id (a UUIDv7 carries its creation time) and never a domain.
- SEQUENCE increments on every change to the time, the place or the status, and on every CANCEL; DTSTAMP is the send time in UTC.
- METHOD:REQUEST for invitations and updates, METHOD:CANCEL with STATUS:CANCELLED when the meeting is cancelled, and METHOD:CANCEL without STATUS to uninvite one person.
- A VTIMEZONE for the meeting's TZID; lines folded at 75 octets; TEXT escaped.
- The `text/calendar; method=…; charset=UTF-8` part in `multipart/alternative` with plain text and HTML.

**Taken from the clients:**
- **Each person gets their own message, naming only themselves as ATTENDEE.** It keeps directors' addresses private from guests, and every client sees itself on the attendee list.
- **The ORGANIZER is the tenant's sending address, never a member.** A secretary as ORGANIZER would get the invite as "their own" meeting in Outlook. RFC 6047 §2.3 allows the email sender to differ from the ORGANIZER anyway.
- **`RSVP=FALSE` on the ATTENDEE and the reply asked for in the app.** Outlook then sends no meeting response. Gmail and Apple may still offer buttons; a reply they send goes to the sending address and is not read (§3.3).
- **REQUEST, not PUBLISH,** because only REQUEST and CANCEL give the update and cancellation semantics in Outlook. PUBLISH (no attendees) is used only for the "Add to my calendar" download.
- **Not everything can be proven in CI.** Google's known-sender setting, Gmail's "Add to calendar" UID behaviour, uninviting one person and Apple's routing can only be seen in real mailboxes. That is the recorded run (Phase 4 Q5 answer (a), §5).

### 3.3 Why RSVP in the app, not REPLY
1. **No inbound mailbox:** processing `METHOD:REPLY` needs an IMAP or SMTP inbound path per tenant, a parser and spoofing defences. AGENTS §3.1 keeps the product free of hosted services; an inbound mail service is a new moving part for every on-prem install.
2. **Replies cannot be trusted:** RFC 6047 §2.2.2 says unsigned iMIP messages "may not be trusted", and none of the target clients signs replies. An attendance answer feeds the quorum forecast and the minutes, so it needs the signed-in user.
3. **Clients disagree:** Outlook sends no reply when `RSVP` is not TRUE; Gmail sometimes shows no buttons at all; Google may not add the event until the person responds by email. A process that depended on replies would fail silently for some directors.
4. **The portals that sync replies use a hosted connector:** OnBoard sends invites from an administrator's own linked calendar through Cronofy, "a third-party calendar integration partner", and pulls RSVPs back, yet still notes that some platforms make attendees email their answer (§4). That model is not open to us (AGENTS §3.1) and is fragile.
5. **One-click links in the email are not safe either:** "anti-spam software often fetches all resources in mail header fields automatically" (RFC 8058 §1, <https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8058>), and security gateways such as Microsoft Defender for Office 365 follow links in bodies, triggering unsubscribe links **[snippet]** (<https://www.suped.com/knowledge/email-deliverability/technical/how-can-i-prevent-microsoft-defender-from-triggering-unwanted-one-click-unsubscribes>). A "Yes / No" link in the invite would record answers nobody gave. The email links to the meeting page; the answer is a signed-in POST.

**What users criticise, and what to do better:**
- **The secretary cannot record an answer given by email or phone** (OnBoard). Directors often tell the secretary; the product must let the secretary record it, saying how and when, and tell the director.
- **Replies reset on every change** (OnBoard resets on title and location changes too). Only a change of date or time affects whether someone can come; a new room or link should not throw away answers.
- **Calendar-connected invites need a hosted connector and an administrator's mailbox** (OnBoard). Ours come from the tenant's own relay and need no one's calendar.
- **Quorum is a single percentage and not per item** (BoardPro). Bank charters need required roles, independents and per-item recusals (ADR-0057, E04).
- **"Maybe" or "Tentative"** is offered by OnBoard and iDeals. For a committee secretary planning quorum, "maybe" is the same as no answer; older directors are better served by three plain choices and the freedom to change them. We do not offer it.

## 5. Attendance and quorum practice (R4, E05 half)
**What the minutes record (CGI, *Minute taking*, April 2017, <https://cgi.org.uk/media/mpjeexxj/minute-taking.pdf>):**
- §5.1: "the names of those directors and other attendees present, identifying the chairman and secretary, and whether anyone was not present for the whole meeting"; a "clear distinction between those directors attending in person, those attending remotely (and how they are doing so), those attending as an alternate and those who are not directors but are in attendance"; "apologies from directors unable to attend".
- §5.1 continued: "Some charities and/or public bodies find it necessary to distinguish between those who have given apologies for the meeting and those who have simply not turned up"; the time zone of the start time if directors dial in from other zones.
- §5.2.1: "an inquorate meeting cannot proceed and would be either adjourned or continued on the basis of a discussion"; it is "the responsibility of the company secretary to be aware whether the meeting is quorate at all times, and advise the chairman should this not be the case".
- §5.3.6: "whether or not the director concerned leaves the room, the minutes should make it clear that he/she took no part in that section of the meeting and, where applicable, could confirm that the meeting remained quorate".

**Robert's Rules** (<https://robertsrules.com/frequently-asked-questions/>): "it is never permissible to transact substantive business in the absence of a quorum"; the continued presence of a quorum is presumed until the chair or a member points out it is gone; "Any member noticing the apparent absence of a quorum can and should make a Point of Order". Emergency action taken without a quorum is the members' own risk and "must be ratified by a meeting with a quorum to be valid" (RONR p. 119, as quoted in <https://www.churchlawandtax.com/stay-legal/governance/qa-are-quorums-always-needed/> **[snippet]** and <https://aprioboardportal.com/board-meeting-without-quorum/> **[snippet]**).

**Remote attendance:** UK model articles art. 10: where directors are does not matter (E01 research §3.3); the Exim charter and the Charity Commission count it with the chair's approval. ADR-0057's `remoteCounts` covers both.

**Alternates:**
- **UK model articles for public companies** (Companies (Model Articles) Regulations 2008, sch. 3, art. 25–26, <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2008/3229/schedule/3>, read through search results **[snippet]**): an alternate acts "in the absence of the alternate's appointor"; art. 26(1) "An alternate director has the same rights, in relation to any directors' meeting … as the alternate's appointor"; art. 26(2) alternates "are subject to the same restrictions as their appointors"; art. 26(3) an alternate who is not a director "may be counted as participating for the purposes of determining whether a quorum is participating (but only if that person's appointor is not participating)", and "No alternate may be counted as more than one director for such purposes".
- **New Hampshire land-use boards** (RSA 673:11 and 673:14, <https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LXIV/673/673-11.htm>, <https://gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/LXIV/673/673-14.htm>): the chair designates an alternate "when a regular member is absent or disqualified", and a disqualified member should tell the chair early "so that an alternate may be requested". These alternates are a **pool** appointed by the body, not a member's personal nominee.
- **The FOMC:** alternates attend for an absent member with "the same status as the member" (E01 research §3.3).
- **HSBC Group Risk Committee:** "two members, including the Chair or their delegate from among the members" (E01 research §3.3): the chair's delegate is another member, not an outsider.

**The E04 question: does a recused member's alternate stand in for that item?**
- Our alternates are **personal** (one per seat, ADR-0056), like company alternate directors, not a pool like the New Hampshire boards.
- Art. 26(2) makes a personal alternate "subject to the same restrictions" as the appointor, and art. 26(3) counts the alternate only while the appointor is not participating. A recused member is still at the meeting; the conflict is theirs, and an alternate they nominated is the person most likely to share it.
- **Answer: no.** A recused member's alternate does not stand in for that item, and is recused from it with them (no papers, not counted). The pool model is not offered in Phase 4; a customer with that charter would bring it as a forum-type setting later.

**Taken from §5:**
- Attendance states: **present in person, present online, apologies, absent (no apologies), in attendance (not a member)**, and per person **arrived during item n** and **left during item n**, as the minutes need them (CGI §5.1).
- **Alternates** count only while their member is absent, count once whoever they stand for, and inherit their member's recusals.
- Quorum is **evaluated live** and shown to the secretary at all times (CGI §5.2.1), **per item** with recusals and departures (CGI §5.3.6), and **recorded with each decision** (E06).
- **No decision without quorum.** A forum type may allow the secretary to record a decision "taken without a quorum, to be ratified", with a reason; it is off by default, and the decision is marked for ratification (Robert's Rules).

## 6. Usability for older, non-technical directors
- W3C WAI, *Developing websites for older people* (<https://www.w3.org/WAI/older-users/developing/>): larger targets; "the purpose of a link can be determined from the link text alone"; no time limits; consistent navigation; errors identified with how to fix them; "changes of context are initiated only by user request".
- E03's principles hold (44 px targets, plain words, one primary action, times in two zones).
- **Taken for E05:**
  - one question on the meeting page, "Will you attend?", with three large answers in words: "Yes, in person", "Yes, online", "No, I send my apologies";
  - the answer can be changed until the meeting starts, and the page says what was recorded and when;
  - the invite email says, in its first line, how to reply ("Please tell us on memo whether you will attend") and never asks the director to use their calendar's buttons;
  - "Add to my calendar" on the meeting page for directors whose mail system holds the invite back;
  - nothing to do at the meeting: the secretary records attendance; the director only sees "You are recorded as present".

## 7. Conclusions for the design
1. **ical4j 4.4.0** writes and validates every invite; tzurl updates off; VTIMEZONE from `java.time`; biweekly 0.6.8 re-parses in tests only (ADR-0063).
2. **One invitation per meeting:** a random UUID, SEQUENCE incremented on time, place, link and status changes and on every CANCEL, DTSTAMP at send time; one message per person naming only them; ORGANIZER the tenant's sending address; `RSVP=FALSE`; no agenda, items or papers; a neutral title per committee type when set (ADR-0063).
3. **Sending through the notification module:** the outbox gains an encrypted calendar part; one MIME layout for every invite; a debounced reconciler job sends each person the latest version only (ADR-0063).
4. **RSVP in the app** with three answers, the secretary able to record answers given by email or phone, answers marked "for the old time" after a reschedule and nothing reset by a new room or link (§4).
5. **Attendance** as an append-only register per person, with arrivals and departures tied to the item in progress, alternates standing in, and corrections as new entries (ADR-0064).
6. **Quorum** live per meeting and per item from ADR-0057's evaluator, with recusals from ADR-0062; alternates count only for the number, never for a required role or a composition attribute, once per person, and never for an item their member is recused from (ADR-0064, amending ADR-0057).
7. **The snapshot** of rule, roster, presence, recusals and result is taken by E06 at each decision; without quorum the decision is refused unless the committee type allows a reasoned override, which marks it for ratification (ADR-0064).
8. **The real-client run** (Q5 answer (a)) covers what CI cannot: Outlook (web and desktop), Gmail with its invitation setting, and optionally Apple Calendar, for request, update, cancel and uninvite (`tasks.md` T9).
